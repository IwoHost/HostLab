Pierogi
Ruskie

The classic. Farmer's cheese, potatoes, onion — the only legitimate filling. Click ingredients and steps to check them off as you cook.

0 / 0
Ingredients
Dough
500 g all-purpose flour
200 ml warm water
1 egg (large)
1 tsp salt
1 tbsp butter (melted)
Filling
700 g starchy potatoes
300 g farmer's cheese (twaróg)
2 large onions
2 tbsp butter
to taste salt and pepper
To serve
3 tbsp butter + onion (for pan-frying)
optional sour cream
Yield
This recipe makes roughly 40–50 pieces depending on how large you cut the rounds. Plenty for 4 servings. Making it for 2? Halve the dough but keep the full filling — you'll eat more than you think.
Instructions
Filling
01
Peel and chop the potatoes. Boil in salted water until fully tender.
~20 min. Check with a fork — they should fall apart, not resist.
02
Dice the onions finely and fry in butter over low heat until golden.
~10 min. You want them soft and sweet, not browned or bitter.
03
Drain the potatoes and mash until smooth. Add the farmer's cheese and fried onion.
Use a masher or ricer — not a mixer. It turns gluey.
04
Season with salt and pepper. Mix into a uniform mass and let it cool completely.
Cold filling won't steam the dough while you seal the edges. Important.
Dough
05
Mound the flour on a work surface and make a well. Add the egg, salt, melted butter, and warm water.
Add water gradually — the dough should be neither dry nor sticky.
06
Knead for 8–10 minutes until the dough is smooth and elastic.
This is the most important step. Under-kneaded dough tears while sealing.
07
Wrap in cling film or cover with a cloth. Rest at room temperature for 30 minutes.
The gluten needs to relax. Skip this and the dough springs back when you roll it.
Shaping and cooking
08
Divide the dough into 4 parts. Roll each thin (2–3 mm) and cut rounds ~8 cm across.
Use a glass or a round cutter. Gather and re-roll the scraps.
09
Place a teaspoon of filling on each round. Fold in half and seal the edges — first press with fingers, then crimp traditionally.
Don't overfill. The edges need to be dry to seal properly.
10
Boil in batches in a large pot of salted water. Once they float, cook 3–4 minutes more.
Don't crowd the pot — they'll stick together. Stir gently at the start.
11
Remove with a slotted spoon. Optionally: pan-fry in butter with onion until golden on both sides.
Boiled is good. Pan-fried is better. This is not an opinion, it is a fact.
12
Serve with sour cream or fried onion on top. Or just plain — also fine.
Freezing
Freeze raw pierogi spread out on a tray so they don't stick, then transfer to a bag. Cook straight from frozen — drop into boiling water, wait until they float, then 4 more minutes. No thawing needed.
